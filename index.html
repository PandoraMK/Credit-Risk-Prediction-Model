1. Objective & Scope

To analyze the relation between gold and silver returns with macroeconomic proxies such as CPI, FOMC, and NFP. This study determines whether macroeconomic factors exert a direct linear influence over precious metal commodity prices using statistical and time-series regression techniques.

2. Data Sources & Variables

CPI (Consumer Price Index): Monthly inflation proxy from FRED.
FOMC (Federal Funds Rate): Monetary policy stance indicator.
NFP (Non-Farm Payrolls): Labor market strength metric.
DXY (US Dollar Index): Exchange rate & currency strength.
S&P 500 (^GSPC): Broader equity market sentiment.
USOIL (Crude Oil): Energy price and economic activity proxy.

5. OLS Regression Results & Findings

The Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression models indicate that macroeconomic variables (CPI, FOMC, NFP) display weak linear relationships with daily gold and silver returns, while market-driven variables like DXY_return and oil_return show statistically significant coefficients ($p < 0.001$).

6. Conclusion & Key Takeaways

Precious metals act less on contemporaneous monthly macroeconomic announcements and more on forward-looking expectations, market sentiment, and safe-haven flows during geopolitical crises. Future expansions of this repo can incorporate lagged variables and non-linear machine learning models.
